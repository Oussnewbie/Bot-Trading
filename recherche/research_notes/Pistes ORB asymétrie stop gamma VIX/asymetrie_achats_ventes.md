# Long/short asymmetry of opening-range breakouts and intraday momentum on US equity indices (Nasdaq 100, S&P 500, NQ/ES, CFDs)

Scope note: literature and practitioner research only. The robot's own figures (from `recherche/critique-v10.md`, 24/11/2026) are quoted only as context in Inferences; they are in-sample (Nasdaq 2010-18/11/2026 fully seen) and are not evidence from outside. Page numbers below are PDF page numbers of the linked file.

Robot context used for comparison (in-sample, descriptive): breakout A wins 52.5 % (long) vs 44.9 % (short) in 2018-2026 and 54.1 % vs 39.5 % in 2010-2017; long-only 2018-2026 = +325 R (max DD 22 R), short-only = +152 R (max DD 43 R), full V10 = +477 R; shorts below the 50-day mean won 42.5 % (2018-2026) and 28.7 % (2010-2017, -0.18 R/trade).

## Q1. Is a long/short asymmetry documented for ORB / intraday momentum on US index futures/ETFs?

### Takeaway
No primary source I found reports long vs short win rates for a 5-minute ORB on QQQ/SPY/NQ/ES. The closest primary evidence (Zarattini-Aziz-Barbon, SPY intraday momentum 2007-2024) finds **both legs profitable, the long leg "slightly" larger**; the QQQ 5-min ORB paper gives only the trade split (51 % long / 49 % short) with no per-side results. The robot's 7-15 point win-rate gap is therefore neither confirmed nor contradicted by published numbers; it is larger than anything the literature hints at.

### Cited Findings
- **Zarattini & Aziz (2023), "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?" — QQQ 5-min ORB, 2016-2023.** Rules: direction = sign of the first 5-min candle (no trade on a doji); entry at open of the 2nd candle; stop = low (long) / high (short) of the first candle; target 10R or end of day; 1 % risk; max leverage 4x; commission $0.0005/share; no slippage (pp. 5-6). Results: 1,795 trades, **51 % long / 49 % short**, annualised Sharpe 1.12, annualised return 31 %, win rate 24 %, average +0.13 R per trade (pp. 8-9). **No separate long vs short performance is reported in the text.** — [Zarattini & Aziz 2023 PDF (copy)](https://wealth-lab.com/api/discussion/download/pdf/6590-ORB-Strategy-pdf); [SSRN 4416622](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622)
- Concretum's page for the same paper: total return 1,484 % (2016-2023, using TQQQ) vs 169 % for passive QQQ; "annualized alpha of 33% (net of commissions)". — [Concretum summary](https://concretumgroup.com/can-day-trading-really-be-profitable/)
- **Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon (2024/2025), "Beat the Market" — SPY intraday momentum, May 2007-Apr 2024, 1-min IQFeed data.** Noise-area boundaries from the average absolute move from the open at each time of day over the previous 14 days; long above the upper band, short below the lower band; decisions only at HH:00 and HH:30; exit at close or opposite band; trailing stop = max(upper band, VWAP) for longs, min(lower band, VWAP) for shorts (pp. 6-13). Net-of-cost result: total return 1,985 %, 19.6 %/yr, Sharpe 1.33 (abstract, p. 2); daily hit ratio about 43 % (pp. 14-15). — [ZAB "Beat the Market" PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content); [SFI working paper 24-97](https://www.sfi.ch/de/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)
- Same paper, FAQ Q5: "Both the long and short legs of the strategy have contributed positively"; "it's expected that the long leg would contribute slightly more ... This is indeed the case" (p. 31; the long-only / short-only equity curves are a chart, numbers not given in text). Short-leg compounded total return read off the FAQ chart: about **330 %** over 2007-2024 (p. 39, my visual reading, approximate). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- Same paper, FAQ Q7: the strategy (long+short) was positive in each of the 10 worst S&P 500 quarters, e.g. Q4 2008 (S&P -22 %, strategy +16 %), Q1 2020 (-19 % / +7 %), Q2 2022 (-16 % / +12 %), Q4 2018 (-14 % / +31 %) (p. 32). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- **Replication of the Zarattini-Aziz ORB on CFDs (Jan 2015-Jun 2026, NQ, SPX, Dow, DAX, FTSE, no parameter search; anonymous mql5 blog, read through a page summary, so independence and method details are not established).** Gross: NQ +0.131 R/trade (t 2.9, hit 23.2 %), SPX +0.119 R (t 2.4), Dow +0.048 R (t 1.1). Net of spread+slippage (NQ 2.5 pts, SPX 0.8, Dow 4.0): NQ **+0.002 R (t 0.0)**, SPX -0.081 R, Dow -0.081 R. **No long/short split reported.** Rule minus random-direction benchmark (net): NQ +0.124 R (t 2.1), SPX +0.092 (t 1.2), Dow +0.021 (t 0.4). — [mql5 blog replication](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- (Search snippets only, pages not opened.) Practitioner sources found (Edgeful 5-min ES ORB, Tradeify ES/NQ study, TradingView scripts, Substack backtests) give no long/short split on the same sample; Edgeful's only directional remark is that ES long breakouts underperformed on Tuesdays (115 trades, day-of-week anecdote). — [Edgeful ES ORB](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/5-minute-opening-range-breakout-es-strategy); [Tradeify ORB](https://tradeify.co/education/opening-range-breakout)
- ZAB cite Rosa (2022) as finding that the Gao et al. intraday momentum predictability declined after 2013 and is regime-dependent (Markov-switching, stronger in volatile regimes) (pp. 3-4). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)

### Inferences
- The only per-leg published evidence (ZAB on SPY) points the same direction as the robot (long leg > short leg) but describes the gap as "slight" and both legs as clearly profitable. Their design (noise bands, entries only from 10:00 on half-hours, VWAP/band trailing stop) differs from a 9:30 5-min ORB with a fixed 3x-range stop, so the shorts' profitability there does not transfer directly.
- The robot's shorts are also net positive (+152 R in 2018-2026, +56 R in 2010-2017), so the pattern "shorts weaker but positive" is consistent with ZAB; what is unusual is the size of the win-rate gap (about 8-15 points on breakout A).
- The QQQ ORB paper's 51/49 trade split shows direction is close to a coin flip at 9:35; any asymmetry must therefore come from what happens after entry (follow-through vs reversal), not from how often each side triggers.

### Gaps
- No primary or practitioner source with a stated method gives long vs short win rate / R per trade for a 9:30 ORB on NQ, ES, QQQ or SPY. The QQQ paper's charts were not inspected for a per-side split (only its text).
- ZAB's long-only and short-only equity curves (p. 31) are a figure without numbers; per-trade R or hit ratio by leg is not published.
- Publication bias: Zarattini and Aziz are affiliated with Concretum Research and Peak Capital Trading / Bear Bull Traders (trading education); their papers are SSRN working papers, not peer-reviewed. The CFD replication on the mql5 blog reports that the classic ORB edge on NQ disappears net of realistic costs.

## Q2. Which mechanisms are proposed (drift, buy-the-dip, short covering, volatility asymmetry, dealer gamma, leveraged ETFs, flows at the open)?

### Takeaway
The literature offers mechanisms for intraday momentum in general (dealer gamma, leveraged-ETF rebalancing, late-informed trading, under-reaction), and one well-documented **directional asymmetry: sell-offs are followed by strong reversals, rallies are not** (NY Fed, S&P futures, overnight horizon). Leveraged-ETF rebalancing is mathematically symmetric in direction. Dealer gamma, as usually modelled (dealers short puts, long calls), predicts that **down-moves should follow through more**, i.e. the opposite of "shorts fail more", unless one also counts the reversal after sell-offs.

### Cited Findings
- **Dealer gamma (Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens, JFE 2021).** 60+ futures, Dec 1974-May 2020; rest-of-day return (r_ROD, from previous close to the last half hour) predicts the last-half-hour return. S&P 500 net gamma exposure (NGE) built from OptionMetrics (1996-2017) and SqueezeMetrics (to May 2020), assuming market makers are short all puts and long all calls: NGE_call = Gamma x OI x 100 x P; NGE_put = Gamma x OI x (-100) x P, summed over strikes and maturities (p. 12, eqs. 13-14). — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [JFE record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403)
- Baltussen et al., footnote 8: "NGE is on average positive for most days"; one explanation is that dealers are short puts and long calls "but markets on average drift up over time in such a manner that the net gamma of the puts drops and the net gamma of calls rises" (1996-May 2020: 2,930 negative-NGE days vs 3,158 positive). — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- ZAB restate the mechanism directionally: near the strikes of calls dealers bought (overwriting), dealers sell rallies and buy dips (dampening); near the strikes of puts dealers sold, they must "enhance intraday movements", amplifying the trend (p. 23). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- **Leveraged ETFs.** Rebalancing demand on day t = NAV_{t-1} x (x^2 - x) x r_t (Cheng & Madhavan), with x = -2, -1, 2, 3...; Cheng & Madhavan estimated LETF rebalancing at 16.8 % (50.2 %) of market-on-close volume on a day the market moved 1 % (5 %) as of Feb 2009; Shum et al. argue the hedging can start up to 30 minutes before the close (Baltussen et al., section 4.2, p. 14). Baltussen et al. find a positive relation between LETF market share and the strength of intraday momentum across markets. — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- **Late-informed trading / infrequent rebalancing.** Gao, Han, Li & Zhou attribute market intraday momentum to Bogousslavsky's (2016) infrequent portfolio rebalancing and to late-informed trading near the close. — [Gao et al. abstract, JFE 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)
- **Under-reaction to news** (disposition effect, limited attention, slow-moving capital) is the explanation ZAB favour for intraday momentum (p. 5). They note the disposition-effect channel works "symmetrically" (slows rises after good news and drops after bad news). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- **Asymmetric reversal after sell-offs vs rallies (S&P 500 futures, 1998-2019).** After sell-offs (bottom tercile of closing order imbalance, same-period annualised closing return -81 %), subsequent overnight returns average 7.6 % (Asian hours) and 12.4 % (European hours) annualised, 7.5 % in the 2-3 a.m. ET window alone; after rallies (top tercile, +81 %), overnight returns average -1.5 % and -5.1 %, with "no reversal effect" at 2-3 a.m. The authors conclude rallies produce much smaller reversals than sell-offs, which generates the overnight drift; they relate it to risk aversion rising more in bad states. — [Liberty Street Economics, May 2021](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2021/05/the-overnight-drift-in-us-equity-returns); [Boyarchenko, Larsen & Whelan, NY Fed SR917](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr917.pdf)
- **0DTE dealer gamma is small and usually long.** Cboe (one year to Sep 2023): market-maker net gamma in SPX 0DTE averages $170 mm-$670 mm through the day; median at 3:30 pm +$173 mm (interquartile -$1.1 bn to +$2.4 bn); implied hedging = 0.04 %-0.17 % of daily S&P futures liquidity; conclusion: no discernible effect on intraday moves. In the 15 Aug 2023 sell-off, dealers were long about $2 bn gamma at 3 pm (dampening the decline). Cboe does not analyse up vs down moves separately. — [Cboe Volatility Insights](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)

### Inferences
- **LETF rebalancing cannot create a long/short asymmetry**: (x^2 - x) > 0 for every leveraged and inverse fund, so rebalancing always trades with the day's move, up or down, and it is concentrated in the last 30 minutes. It adds late-day momentum in both directions.
- **Dealer gamma, as modelled, predicts the opposite of the robot's pattern for follow-through**: below spot dealers are short (put) gamma and amplify falls; above spot they are long (call) gamma and dampen rises. If anything, down-breakouts that get going should run further. Combined with NGE being positive "most days" in rising markets, the gamma story predicts: on most days (positive gamma) both breakouts tend to fail; on the fewer negative-gamma days, momentum is strong, and those days are disproportionately down days. That fits "shorts win less often but have large winners on crash days" (robot shorts are still +152 R at a 41.6 % win rate), but it does not explain a lower short win rate on its own.
- **The best-documented directional mechanism is the asymmetric reversal after sell-offs** (NY Fed): selling pressure is absorbed and reversed, buying pressure is not. The NY Fed evidence concerns the close-to-overnight horizon, not 9:35-15:50, so applying it to opening down-breakouts ("opening sales bought back within the session", as the critique hypothesises) is an extrapolation, though a plausible one.
- Buy-the-dip retail flows, short covering and volatility asymmetry (down moves faster but mean-reverting) are frequently asserted in practitioner commentary, but I found no primary source quantifying them at the 9:30-16:00 horizon on US indices (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No primary source found that measures intraday (9:30-16:00) reversal after down-opens vs up-opens on ES/NQ, or "buy-the-dip" order flow at the intraday horizon.
- No primary source retrieved on volatility asymmetry of intraday index moves (speed/duration of down vs up moves) — leverage-effect literature exists but was not checked for intraday breakouts.
- Baltussen et al. include NQ futures in their sample (9:30-16:00 hours) but I did not extract an NQ-specific coefficient; they report no test of up-day vs down-day momentum (searched the full text for asymmetry terms: none).
- Dim, Eraker & Vilkov (2024, "0DTEs: Trading, Gamma Risk and Volatility Propagation") is reported by secondary sources to find that higher 0DTE gamma is associated with lower intraday volatility; the paper itself was not retrieved. Cboe is an interested party (it lists 0DTE options).
- Bondarenko & Muravyev ("Market return around the clock") and Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka (intraday periodicity) were not retrieved.

## Q3. Are regular-session returns of US indices flat/negative vs positive overnight, and what does that imply for long vs short intraday trades?

### Takeaway
Yes on average, historically: most of the US equity premium was earned overnight; the regular session earned little (S&P futures: morning negative on average until noon, flat until about 3 pm, positive only in the last 75 minutes). But this drift is far too small per day to explain a 7-15 point win-rate gap on its own, and the morning part of it would favour shorts, not longs. The overnight drift has also largely disappeared in 2021-2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Intraday profile of S&P 500 futures (1998-2019):** the 9:30 open "is preceded by large negative returns, a pattern we call 'opening reversal,' with returns continuing to be negative on average until 12 p.m."; opening reversals are "only statistically significantly negative on Thursdays and Fridays"; intraday returns "are then roughly flat until 3 p.m." and "only positive on average during the last seventy-five minutes of trading". — [Liberty Street Economics, May 2021](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2021/05/the-overnight-drift-in-us-equity-returns)
- **Overnight drift concentration:** Boyarchenko, Larsen & Whelan (RFS 2023, 36(9):3502-3547) report that almost 100 % of the US equity premium is earned in the 1-hour window 2:00-3:00 a.m. ET (abstract-level claim, from the working paper / search snippet, not verified in the text). This conflicts in size with the same team's 2026 post, where the 2-3 a.m. window is "more than 60 percent" (3.7 of 5.9 %/yr) of the close-to-close return over 1998-2020 (next bullet); the difference may come from premium vs total return or sample, not checked. — [NY Fed SR917](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr917.pdf); [RePEc RFS record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:36:y:2023:i:9:p:3502-3547.)
- **2021-2025 change:** the 2-3 a.m. window "previously generated roughly 3.7 percent per annum", "more than 60 percent of the contract's 5.9 percent annualized close-to-close return" (1998-2020), but "has averaged close to zero since 2021" (E-mini S&P, Jan 2021-Dec 2025). Explanation offered: lower dispersion of end-of-day order imbalance (std of end-of-day RSV fell from 6.5 % to 2.9 %). The post gives no 9:30-16:00 return figures. — [Liberty Street Economics, July 2026, "The Disappearing Overnight Drift"](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)
- **US market return decomposition 1997Q1-2023Q4:** average quarterly overnight return 1.83 % vs average quarterly intraday return 0.25 % (Lou, Polk & Skouras, "The Day Destroys the Night, Night Extends the Day: A Clientele Perspective on Equity Premium Variation", Table I, section 3.4). The same authors report that past smoothed overnight market returns negatively forecast later close-to-close returns, "primarily through intraday mean reversion" (abstract, per search snippet). — [Lou, Polk & Skouras working paper PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/loud/LouPolkSkouras.pdf); [FMG DP961](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-07/DP961.pdf)
- Cliff, Cooper & Gulen (2008) is cited by later work as finding, on S&P 500 data 1993-2003, that the equity premium is earned entirely overnight (secondary citation only; original not retrieved). — [Boyarchenko et al., CEPR DP14462](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP14462.pdf); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/overnight-momentum-vs-intraday-momentum/)
- CXO Advisory (SPY, Jan 1993-Aug 2017): overnight returns exceed intraday returns on average, but the effect is unstable across subperiods (search snippet). — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/buy-at-the-close-and-sell-at-the-open)
- ZAB note that an overnight gap down followed by a stable regular session may not trigger their momentum model, which is why they say it is not a straightforward hedge (FAQ Q8, pp. 32-33). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)

### Inferences
- **Order-of-magnitude check (my own arithmetic, not a source):** an intraday drift of 0.25 %/quarter is about 0.4 bp per day. Even assuming Nasdaq's whole ~15 %/yr return accrued intraday (about 6 bp/day) against a daily volatility of about 1.3 %, the drift shifts the probability of closing above the entry by roughly (0.06/1.3) x 0.4, about 2 points: at most about +1-2 points for longs and -1-2 for shorts. A pure drift explanation therefore accounts for at most a few points of the robot's 7-15 point gap. Two other explanations remain: (a) conditional behaviour (reversals after down-breaks, path/stop effects), consistent with the robot's finding that the trend regime does not explain the gap; (b) **distribution shape with no drift at all**: if intraday index moves are negatively skewed (many small rises, few large falls), longs win more often and shorts win less often but bigger, even at zero mean. Measuring the R distribution of long vs short trades (Q5) separates (a) from (b).
- ZAB's FAQ Q8 implies (my reading, not their wording) that on gap-down crash days much of the fall can happen overnight, before any intraday breakout system can act.
- **The direction of the morning drift is against the "drift favours longs" story**: on S&P futures 1998-2019, 9:30-12:00 was negative on average, which should help a 9:35 short. Only the late positive drift (roughly 3:00-4:15 pm) helps a long held to 15:50. The robot exits at 15:50, so its longs capture most of that late drift and its shorts pay it.
- Because the overnight drift has faded since 2021 (and Lou-Polk-Skouras find intraday mean reversion of overnight moves), the overnight/intraday split is not a stable structural reason to treat shorts differently going forward.

### Gaps
- No primary source found with Nasdaq-100 (QQQ/NQ) open-to-close vs close-to-open decompositions or with the share of days where QQQ/SPY close above the open. A practitioner page reports a 51.7 % "intraday win rate" for SPY over 8,405 days with the overnight path compounding $100 to $1,464 vs $113 intraday, but the definition was not visible and it was not verified. — [thetrading.tools weekday analysis](https://www.thetrading.tools/weekday-analysis)
- Kelly & Clark (2011) and Lou, Polk & Skouras (2019, JFE, "A tug of war") were not retrieved directly; the LSE URL above hosts the later clientele paper.
- The NY Fed intraday profile ends in 2019; no figure for 2020-2026 intraday hour-by-hour returns was found.

## Q4. Which conditions are documented to make short intraday breakouts work better (VIX, negative gamma, bear markets, gap, news, first-hour volume)?

### Takeaway
The one primary source that tested conditions **on short trades specifically** (ZAB, SPY 2007-2024) found **no per-trade link between short PnL and VIX (p = 0.91)**, and showed that **most short-side total profit came from days above the 100/150/200-day SMA**; per-trade results by trend regime are not published, so the per-trade evidence against trend filters on shorts is the robot's own (50-day mean). The documented conditions are **direction-agnostic**: intraday momentum exists only when dealers are net short gamma (NGE < 0), and is stronger on high-volatility, high-volume, macro-news and recession days. None is shown to fix shorts in particular.

### Cited Findings
- **VIX and shorts (ZAB, FAQ Q19):** regression of each short trade's PnL (bps) on "the average VIX level on the same day": "no significant relationship", p-value of beta 0.91 (p. 38). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- **VIX threshold (ZAB, FAQ Q20):** compounded total return of all short trades restricted to VIX above a threshold, read off the bar chart (approximate): all about 332 %, VIX > 15 about 272 %, > 20 about 91 %, > 25 about 22 %, > 30 about 9 %; text: "limiting short trades to high volatility environments does not benefit overall compounded performance" (p. 39). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- **Bear-market filter (ZAB, FAQ Q21):** short-leg compounded total return split by price vs SMA, chart reading (approximate): SMA100 bear about 43 % vs bull about 202 %; SMA150 about 50 % vs 188 %; SMA200 about 59 % vs 171 %; text: traders who take short intraday trend exposures only during bear markets "are sacrificing a substantial portion of overall profitability" (p. 39). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- **VIX at the open, whole strategy (not shorts):** text: "when the VIX is above 6, the Sharpe Ratio is approximately 1.50; remarkably, when the VIX exceeds 40, the Sharpe Ratio escalates to an impressive 3.50", then decreases at higher levels with fewer observations (pp. 17-18). The 1.50 for VIX > 6 (effectively all days) does not match the full-sample Sharpe of 1.33 in the abstract: probable typo or a different threshold; not resolved. — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- **Gamma proxy, whole strategy:** daily strategy return (bps) regressed on SPY 5-day RSI at the previous close: beta -3.25, p = 0.001; lower RSI (recent decline, hypothesised lower dealer gamma) goes with higher momentum returns. The authors state that estimating gamma precisely is beyond their scope; RSI is their hypothesised proxy (pp. 23-24). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- **Net gamma sign, S&P 500 futures, Jan 1996-May 2020 (Baltussen et al., Table 7):** regression of last-half-hour return on r_ROD: if NGE_{t-1} >= 0, beta 0.82 (t 1.03), R^2 0.05 %; if NGE_{t-1} < 0, **beta 6.63 (t 4.78), R^2 3.58 %** (coefficients x100; p. 13). "On days with positive NGE ... there is no significant intraday momentum"; momentum is stronger the more negative NGE is (interaction NGE x r_ROD: -123.04, t -3.42). Results also hold with Gao et al.'s predictor and with puts only. — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- **Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (JFE 2018), SPY 1993-2013:** the first half-hour return "as measured from the previous day's market close" predicts the last half-hour return; predictability is stronger on more volatile days, higher-volume days, recession days and major macroeconomic news release days. — [Gao et al., JFE 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)
- **First-candle direction gating** (ORB papers): trade only in the direction of the first 5-min candle; no trade on a doji (Zarattini & Aziz QQQ, p. 5; same rule in the Stocks-in-Play paper, which is single stocks and out of scope here). — [Zarattini & Aziz 2023 PDF](https://wealth-lab.com/api/discussion/download/pdf/6590-ORB-Strategy-pdf); [Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz Stocks in Play PDF](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/3c2989c4-688d-4d78-8a71-f02690990d51/content)

### Inferences
- The robot already tried gap, first-half-hour momentum, 8:30 news, MA/RSI/MACD filters and "the Dow confirms the Nasdaq" without durable effect (critique-v10.md §3). The literature does not offer a different short-specific condition; on VIX it finds no per-trade link for shorts, and on trend it shows only totals (most short profit on above-SMA days).
- **The only untested, literature-backed condition is dealer gamma sign (NGE < 0)**, and it is symmetric: it predicts momentum for both sides on negative-gamma days and no momentum on positive-gamma days. Applying it to shorts only is an extrapolation. Counter-evidence lowers the prior: in both sources negative gamma goes with recent declines (Baltussen footnote 8; ZAB's low-RSI proxy), and the robot's crude post-decline proxies (price below the 50-day mean, RSI filters) did not help shorts, with 2010-2017 worse. That does not refute a measured NGE, but it makes a large gain for shorts less likely.
- **Look-ahead traps in these conditions:** ZAB Q19 uses the same-day average VIX (unknowable at 9:35; their null result stands anyway); Gao et al.'s "recession days" are NBER-dated after the fact and their predictor includes the overnight gap (previous close to 10:00), so it is not a 9:30 opening-range signal; NGE needs end-of-day open interest, so only NGE_{t-1} is usable at 9:35; SqueezeMetrics history should be checked for revisions or back-fill before use.
- The ZAB SMA and VIX bars show **total** compounded return, which mechanically falls when trades are removed; they do not show that filtered shorts are worse per trade. Only Q19 (p = 0.91) is per-trade evidence.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives exact rule definitions for short-only conditions such as negative gamma at the open, VIX term structure (VIX > VIX3M), gap-down size or first-hour volume, with per-trade results for shorts on US index futures.
- Whether a public NGE / GEX history (e.g. SqueezeMetrics) exists back to 2010 with values timestamped before 9:30 was not verified.
- No source tested news days or gap direction separately for long vs short ORB trades.

## Q5. Which simple, testable rule (no optimisation) would best capture this?

### Takeaway
The literature supports no specific short filter. The defensible options without curve-fitting are, in order: (1) **long-only** (zero parameters, matches the robot's 16-year pattern and the "long leg larger" finding of ZAB); (2) **shorts at a fixed reduced risk**, a blend whose result can be computed from already-seen long-only and short-only figures; (3) **shorts only when the first 5-min candle closes down** (the ORB papers' direction gate, the only ORB-specific a-priori rule with NQ CFD evidence; symmetric, and possibly already in the trial register); (4) **shorts only when dealer gamma is negative (NGE_{t-1} < 0)**, the one threshold-free regime condition in the literature, with a lowered prior. Rules 3-4 must be written in advance and judged once on unseen data. Nothing in the literature supports a different stop or target for shorts.

### Cited Findings
- ZAB use identical rules for both legs (same bands, same VWAP/band trailing stop) and report both legs profitable, long slightly more (pp. 9-13, 31). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- ZAB: filtering shorts by VIX or by SMA 100/150/200 reduced total short-side profit; only the VIX test is per-trade (p = 0.91) (pp. 38-39). — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)
- Baltussen et al.: intraday momentum only when NGE_{t-1} < 0; none when NGE_{t-1} >= 0 (Table 7). — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- ZAB: strategy (long+short) positive in each of the 10 worst S&P quarters 2008-2022 (p. 32); the split by leg in those quarters is not published.
- Zarattini & Aziz QQQ ORB: trade only in the direction of the first 5-min candle (p. 5); CFD replication on NQ: rule minus random direction +0.124 R net (t 2.1), i.e. the first candle carried information on NQ 2015-2026. — [Zarattini & Aziz 2023 PDF](https://wealth-lab.com/api/discussion/download/pdf/6590-ORB-Strategy-pdf); [mql5 blog replication](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235) — [ZAB PDF](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/a99aba00-f967-49b3-aceb-f544dc386e0b/content)

### Inferences
- **Rule 1, long-only.** No parameter. Robot in-sample: +325 R / DD 22 R vs +477 R / DD 32 R (2018-2026), about 68 % of profit kept. Cost: about 30 % of profit, and possibly some protection on crash days (not shown by the sources: ZAB's crisis-quarter results are long+short combined, and most of their short profit came from above-SMA days). Risk: this choice is itself made after seeing 16 years of Nasdaq data, so it is a candidate, not a proof; it can only be confirmed on data from 19/11/2026 onward (or once on the Dow 2024-2026 vault, pre-registered).
- **Rule 2, shorts at half risk** (e.g. 0.125 % instead of 0.25 %). One fixed constant chosen a priori, not optimised. On already-seen data its total is simply long + 0.5 x short (about +401 R in 2018-2026); only the drawdown needs checking. It keeps part of the crisis exposure while reducing the side with the lower win rate. It does not raise the win rate, so it does not meet the user's "lose less often" goal by itself.
- **Rule 3, shorts only if the first 5-min candle (9:30-9:35) closes below its open** (no parameter; doji = no short). The ORB papers apply it to both sides, so it is symmetric, not a short-specific fix; check the trial register first, since V10 may already be equivalent or it may have been tested among the ~2,000 trials. Its NQ support is the mql5 replication (+0.124 R net vs random direction, t 2.1).
- **Rule 4, shorts only if NGE_{t-1} < 0** (sign only, no threshold to tune). Literature-backed (Baltussen) and untested by the robot. Caveats: the evidence is about last-half-hour momentum on the S&P, not a 9:35 ORB on the Nasdaq; the effect is symmetric (a consistent version would also gate longs); it needs a gamma history known before 9:30; the robot's post-decline proxies did not help shorts (lower prior, see Q4). Write the hypothesis and criterion in `notes/idees-strategies.md` before looking, and test on the Dow vault or on new data only.
- **Not recommended from the literature:** VIX-level or trend (SMA) filters on shorts (ZAB: no per-trade VIX link and most short profit on above-SMA days; the robot: no per-trade gain below the 50-day mean); different stops or targets for shorts (no source; robot already tested fixed targets and time exits without success).
- To understand rather than filter, the most informative next measurement on already-seen data (descriptive, not for tuning) would be the **R distribution of short vs long trades** (are short winners larger, i.e. low hit rate offset by big crash-day wins?) and **the time of day at which failed shorts are stopped** (a morning reversal would point to the NY Fed "sell-off reversal" mechanism; an afternoon stop-out would point to the late-day positive drift and dealer flows near the close).

### Gaps
- No published study compares long-only vs long+short vs gated-short ORB on Nasdaq 100 with out-of-sample validation.
- The size of the dealer-gamma effect on a 9:35 Nasdaq breakout (rather than the S&P last half hour) is unknown.
- Every rule above is judged against robot figures that are in-sample; per project discipline, any of them is a candidate until tested on unseen data.
